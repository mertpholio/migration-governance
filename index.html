Beyond the map

Why do NGOs exist in this system?

States make the decisions and UN agencies set standards, but neither can sit with a family every week. NGOs fill that space, mostly with money that flows down from donors.

What NGOs bring

  • Reach and trust. Staff speak the language and work in the neighbourhood; people come to them before they go to an office.
  • Specialist skills. Legal aid, case management, psychosocial support, interpretation.
  • Speed. A project can start in weeks; a law or a new public service takes years.
  • Independent eyes. Monitoring, reports and strategic litigation that hold states to their commitments.

The tensions

  • Donor dependency. Priorities follow the funding call, not always the community’s need.
  • Short projects. Often one to three years; when the grant ends, the service can stop.
  • Reporting load. Indicators, beneficiary counts and audits take a large share of staff time.
  • Two roles at once. Partner of the state and critic of the state, sometimes in the same month.
Working in the field

Who does what: jobs in migration governance

The same roles appear in UN agencies, international NGOs and local associations. Titles change; the work is recognisable.

NGO · UN

Case worker / social worker

Meets families, assesses needs, refers them to health, legal or education services and follows up.

Languages, interviewing, referral pathways

UN · NGO · bar associations

Protection officer / legal aid

Explains status and rights, prepares applications, follows detention and deportation cases.

Refugee law, national migration law, casework

NGO · UN · donors

Monitoring & evaluation officer

Builds indicators, runs surveys, checks whether the project reached its targets and writes the evidence base.

Survey design, statistics, data tools

NGO · INGO

Project coordinator

Turns a grant into a work plan, manages staff and budget, and reports to the donor.

Planning, budgeting, donor rules

NGO · INGO

Grants & proposal writer

Reads funding calls, writes proposals with a logical framework, and keeps the organization funded.

Writing, logframes, budgets

NGO · think tanks · academia

Researcher / advocacy officer

Documents what happens in the field and turns it into reports, policy briefs and media work.

Research methods, writing, media

Public sector

Migration expert (state)

Registers applicants, interviews for status decisions, runs integration programmes.

Public exam, law, languages

UN · INGO

Programme / field officer

Oversees implementing partners, visits sites, and connects field reality to headquarters.

English, field experience, coordination
